# analysis

Layer L8 (`music-analysis`). Path `src/analysis/`, tests `tests/analysis/`. One of two mutually-restricted L8 siblings (with `generation`); see `docs/modules/l8-notes.md` for the sibling rule and shared L8 notes.

## Provides

`Analyzer`, `AnalysisResult`, `FeatureExtraction`, and twelve concrete analyzers (`IntervalAnalysis`, `ScaleAnalysis`, `VoiceLeadingAnalysis`, `CadenceDetection`, `CadenceClassification`, `PhraseSegmentation`, `PhraseBoundaryDetection`, `PeriodDetection`, `SentenceDetection`, `FormStructureDetection`, `ThematicReturnDetection`, plus the `SentenceContinuationMatching` procedure it consumes).

## Notes

An `Analyzer` never returns a bare conclusion; it derives owned `Interpretation`/`Evidence` objects (L6) into an `AnalysisResult`, which owns them (there is nowhere else for them to live once `analyze()` returns). `PitchIdentity` carries no tuning-system semantics, so no analyzer can derive its own measurements (semitone displacement, scale/chord/key fit, motion classification, boundary confidence) from raw pitch tokens; each takes that dimension as an already-computed input field (scale degrees, tonic-relative pitch classes, and so on).

**Real (wired to `frameworks/common-practice-minimal/`):**

- `ChordIdentification` (moved to the chain: `chain/ursatz_chain/subset_chord_analyzer.h`, `chain_subset_chord_analyze`) -- given a Sonority's scale degrees, produces a real, populated `Chord` (L6) naming one of common-practice-minimal-v1's seven diatonic triads or four seventh qualities, or reports a failed analysis on no match. This is the subset-match path, kept unchanged for its current callers (Analyzer, GUI): extra degrees are ignored, the first match wins and ties are logged to stderr, so it tolerates one non-chord tone per beat but not two or more. Weighted identification is the replacement path, below.
- **Weighted chord identification** (`frameworks/common-practice-minimal/weighted_chord_identification.h`) -- the new way to choose a chord for a segment of sounding notes, over the adaptive sonority segments (`semantics/sonority_segmentation.h`). It weighs what sounds, scores the diatonic candidates of the caller's key in its own mode (14 in a major key; in a minor key the 26 diatonic triads and sevenths of natural, harmonic and melodic minor, with the raised V and vii-dim winning an exact tie), reports a best chord, a runner-up and an uncertainty, and labels the leftover tones with `NonChordTone`. It is a new function beside the subset-match ones; callers move over later.
  - **Input.** A plain per-member struct (`CommonPracticeMember`: note id, pitch as a semitone number, held flag, duration sounding in the segment, the `BeatStrength` of the attack, optional Voice id, onset). common-practice-minimal includes no other framework, so the caller computes each `BeatStrength` with standard-meter and passes it in, the same injection pattern the cadence ranking uses.
  - **Weights.** A member's weight is its duration sounding in the segment times a factor: the beat-strength factor of its attack (strong 1.0, medium 0.7, weak 0.4; `BeatStrength` has three values, evenly spaced from the downbeat's 1.0 to the weakest 0.4), or the held factor 0.6 for a note held over from an earlier segment. The bass (the lowest sounding pitch, every member at that pitch) is multiplied by the bass weight 1.5. A pitch class's weight is the sum over its members. All are configurable (`CommonPracticeWeightedConfig`), and all arithmetic is IEEE double summed in member order, so a result is deterministic for a given input.
  - **Scoring.** The candidates are the seven diatonic triads and the seven diatonic sevenths of a MAJOR key (each root takes the seventh quality its scale gives: I and IV major7, ii iii vi minor7, V dominant7, vii half-diminished7). Fully-diminished sevenths, chromatic and applied chords are not candidates, and a minor key is an error (the vocabulary is the major-key set; minor labelling is Roman numeral work for standard-tonality). `score = chord-tone weight / total weight - 0.15 if the chord's third has no weight + 0.05 if the bass pitch class is a chord tone` (defaults, configurable). A score runs from below 0 to 1.05.
  - **Tie-breaks.** Scores within 1e-9 are tied. A tie goes to the chord whose root is the bass (root position), then to the triad over the seventh, then to the lower scale degree, so the ranking is total.
  - **Runner-up and uncertainty.** The runner-up is the best candidate on a different root from the best (a triad and the seventh built on it are one harmony, so the seventh of a bare triad does not count as its rival). `uncertainty = runner-up score / best score`, clamped to [0, 1] (1 if the best score is not positive): higher means less certain, and 1 is a dead tie. A best score below `min_coverage` (default 0.5) is no match: no chords are built and no tones labeled, though the scores are still reported. The Chords are the existing Chord/Interpretation shape; the Interpretation's scalar confidence is a rational in millionths: the best chord's is `1 - uncertainty` and the runner-up's is the uncertainty (its plausibility relative to the best). A seventh Chord carries the seventh quality only, so the chord's root is in the candidate beside it.
  - **Leftover tones.** Members whose pitch class is not in the chosen chord are labeled with the existing `NonChordTone` enum, in a second pass over the sequence (a tone's neighbors and the next chord must be in view): passing (step in, step out, same direction), neighbor (step in, step out, opposite directions), appoggiatura (leap in, step out, opposite direction), escape tone (step in, leap out, opposite direction), suspension (held from the previous segment, then a step down), anticipation (its pitch sounds again in the next segment, where it is a chord tone) and pedal (the same note is the bass of three or more consecutive segments). A step is 1 or 2 semitones, a leap more than 2. Neighbors are the same voice's previous and next notes by onset when there is a Voice id, otherwise the nearest pitch in the adjacent segments (two equally near neighbors are ambiguous). A tone matching more than one rule, or none, is unclassified.
  - **Known limits.** Diatonic candidates only. A passing tone that completes a seventh chord can pull the choice to that seventh. A retardation, a double neighbor and a changing tone are not recognised and are left unclassified. Candidates whose root does not sound are not ranked (otherwise a bare triad ties with the seventh chord above it). Chords in context (frameworks/common-practice-minimal/README.md): a melody note over unchanged harmony is merged into its chord at segmentation, an incomplete segment takes the previous or next chord that holds its pitch classes, and an arpeggio inside one beat is read as one chord; the bass weight (1.1) applies to the bass pitch class. Measured on Field Nocturne No. 1 (61 annotated segments of the opening), the identified chord has the annotated root for 78.7%, unchanged by the context reading and below the 90% target (not asserted); the I - V7 - I progression holds. Errors are mostly a vii or iii read where a V7 or I was marked, on beats whose bass sounded in an earlier beat and is not held into them. On the chorale harness root agreement is 83.8% (82.8% before). Evaluation on Field Nocturne No. 1 uses draft hand marks (`tests/fixtures/annotations/field1_harmony.txt`) not yet checked against the score.
- **Outer-voice extraction** (`analysis/outer_voice.h`) -- the soprano (highest) and bass (lowest) sounding line over the adaptive sonority segments. Theory-neutral: it reads `PitchNumericValue` heights only and takes an injected beat-grid provider (for the smoothing windows); it knows no key or framework.
- **Soprano at every attack** (`outer_voice_attack_soprano`, `analysis/outer_voice.h`) -- the indices of the notes of an `AnalysisNoteSet` that are the highest note sounding at their own onset (the later attack among equal pitches), kept when attacked there. Notes of unknown pitch are skipped. Exact `Rational` times; an overflow is `OUTER_VOICE_ERR_OVERFLOW`. Port of Analyzer `motive_pipeline.c` `find_soprano`; the chain's motive soprano calls it on the notes of the measures it reads.
- **Progression patterns** (`analysis/progression_patterns.h`) -- the chord progressions a piece repeats: held chords merged, runs of 2 to 6 chords inside blocks (a NULL label or group -1 ends a block, a change of group ends one), patterns that occur at least twice, a pattern dropped when a longer one with as many occurrences contains it, ordered by occurrences, length, then earliest. Labels are compared as text; no theory. Port of Analyzer `progression_pipeline.c` without file-scope state.
- **Motive link choosing and grouping** (`motive_link_candidates`, `motive_link_groups`, `analysis/motive_link.h`, `docs/modules/motive-link.md`) -- the pairs of sketches in different pieces worth comparing (the nearest few above the gate), and the groups the linked pairs join. Port of Analyzer `motive_links.c` `collect_pairs` and `build_groups` without the document fields.
  - **Extraction.** Per segment, the soprano is the highest sounding note and the bass the lowest. A segment with no sounding note has an absent entry (no pitch), which is distinct from a note that happens to be the highest; silent time has no entry. Pitch ties pick the most recently attacked note, then the lowest note id.
  - **Smoothing** (each rule configurable, all on by default). (1) Gap bridging: a run of absent entries shorter than the window (default 1 beat; a gap of exactly one beat is not "under one beat") between two present entries stays a rest. (2) Soprano register guard: a segment whose soprano jumps more than the leap limit (default 12 semitones) from the previous line note, after a gap shorter than the window, is dropped as a register intrusion. (3) Bass register guard: the same for the bass.
  - **Limits.** No voice-crossing awareness, no inner voices, one line per extreme.
- **Outer scale degrees** (`frameworks/common-practice-minimal/outer_scale_degrees.h`) -- soprano and bass `ScaleDegree` Interpretations per segment, relative to the caller's key, each with the required `FrameworkReference` and confidence 1/1. Labels are "1".."7" with "b"/"#" for chromatic degrees (major and natural-minor tables); the tonic is a pitch class, 0-11.
- **Voice separation** (`analysis/voice_separation.h`) -- assigns each note of a stream to a voice index (0 = top) by pitch height and time alone. Theory-neutral and rule-based: it reads `PitchNumericValue` heights, knows no key or framework, and takes an injected beat grid (`BeatGridProvider` with its `MeterMap`) only to measure rests. It returns `{note id, stream, voice, confidence, chord-member flag}` per note and a voice count per stream; it builds no `Voice` objects and the importer's per-track `Voice` is unchanged.
  - **Streams.** The caller gives each note's stream (`stream_of_note`; NULL = one stream) and streams are separated independently. Recommended setup: one stream per importer track, because in piano files a track is usually a hand, not a voice; one stream for the whole file is the single-stream mode (used on Bach 114 and the chorale). The kernel cannot see tracks itself.
  - **Algorithm and defaults** (`VoiceSeparationConfig`). (1) Voice count = the most notes sounding at once, capped at `max_voices` (default 4, at most 8). (2) Voices are ordered top to bottom and do not cross unless `allow_crossing` (default false). (3) At each onset the attacked notes go to the free voices (a voice holding a sounding note keeps it) so the total pitch distance from each voice's last pitch is least; without crossing the attacked notes keep their pitch order, and a note on the wrong side of a held note counts as a violation that ranks before distance, so it is chosen only when nothing avoids it. (4) A voice with no note to take rests. (5) Continuity across rests: a voice whose last note ended up to `rest_hold` beats ago (default 2, inclusive; beat length from the beat grid) keeps its last pitch as its reference, so the same voice resumes after a short rest; after that the voice is free and is measured from its nominal pitch, the middle of its band when the stream's pitch range is split into equal bands, so it re-enters by register. (6) More attacked notes than free voices: the extras (chosen by repeatedly dropping the lower note of the closest pair) join the nearest voice as chord members and are flagged. Ties: lower total distance, then the more recently sounding voice, then the lower note id.
  - **Confidence** is in [0, 1] from the cost margin of the onset's assignment, (second best - best) / second best; 1 when only one assignment was possible, 0 when two tie. All notes attacked at one onset share it.
  - **Gate** (Law 12): rule-based separation must reach 90% of notes in the right voice on the four-part chorale fixture and 80% on the corpus checks, or a statistical method may be considered later, reported as an Observation with Uncertainty and never as a Rule. This module implements no statistical method; it measures and reports. The chorale fixture (`tests/fixtures/annotations/chorale_satb.txt`) is a hand-written draft in Bach's idiom after "Aus meiner Herzens Grunde" (BWV 269), not a transcription of an edition. Bach 114 has one track, so its check is the draft annotation `bach114_outer_voices.txt` (voice 0 exactly where the written part is the melody). Mozart 545, Field 1 and Clementi 36 are printed, not asserted. **Result: the gate passes.** Chorale 120/120 (100%; soprano, alto, tenor and bass each 100%), which is an easy texture (four voices always sounding together, no crossings, so a pitch-ordered match is already right). Bach 114 measures 1-8: 93/110 (84.5%; melody 55/72, accompaniment 38/38). All 17 misses are written melody notes placed in a lower voice, after a melodic leap down into the register of a resting inner voice that `rest_hold` still keeps, and in the fast ornaments of measures 2 and 6. Printed only: Mozart 545 gives 4 voices with 0.1% chord-member notes and mean confidence 0.62; Field 1 gives 4 voices (0.6% members, confidence 0.71) and, merged to one stream, the right-hand track spreads over voices 0-3 (186/129/68/24 notes) because the hands overlap in register; Clementi 36 gives 3 voices (confidence 0.67).
  - **Known limits.** Arpeggiated accompaniment (a broken chord is a series of single notes that the nearest-pitch rule can hand to the wrong voice, or to the melody); voice crossing (the default forbids it, and allow_crossing only changes the choice against held notes, since a minimum-distance matching of free voices is already pitch-ordered); unisons and overlaps (two voices on one pitch tie in cost and fall to recency then note id, and a new attack over a still-sounding note in the same voice goes to another voice); more than `max_voices` notes (the extras are flagged members, not voices); the voice count is the stream-wide maximum, so one wide chord gives the whole stream that many voices; no voice sounds again after being chosen as a member; a hand-recorded track with notes held past the next onset can look like extra voices.
- Measured against draft hand-written melody and bass of Bach 114 and Mozart 545 (`tests/fixtures/annotations/*_outer_voices.txt`, unverified transcriptions): agreement is reported by `tests/analysis/test_outer_voices_written_parts.c`. Bach 114 is declared 4/4 but is really 3/4, which the draft marks account for. Measured on measures 1-8: Bach 114 soprano 35/36 (97.2%), bass 33/36 (91.7%); Mozart 545 soprano 46/48 (95.8%), bass 43/48 (89.6%). Soprano meets the 95% target; bass does not (asserted at 90% and 85%). Every bass miss is a segment where the written left hand rests (expected absent) and a right-hand note is adopted as the lowest sounding note; the remaining soprano misses are the same effect in reverse. Defaults are not tuned to these fixtures.
- **Local key regions** (`frameworks/common-practice-minimal/key_regions.h`) -- a key per stretch of the piece instead of one for the whole piece. It is a NEW function beside `KeyEstimation` and `key_identification.h`, which are unchanged and still give the whole-piece answer to their current callers. It lives in common-practice-minimal, next to chord identification, because the pivot step needs its chords. No Roman numerals, applied-chord labels or harmonic function: those are later work.
  - **Input.** Plain structs, no other framework: each adaptive segment's time window, its `CommonPracticeWeights` (the L2.5 pitch-class weighting), and its members (for pivots). Key signature events come in as `{position, sharps_flats}`, filled by the caller from `midi_key_signature_scan_all`.
  - **Windowing.** A sliding window of `window_measures` measures (default 4) with a hop of 1 measure; a piece shorter than the window gets one window. Fixed windows stand in for phrases until phrase detection exists (L3.6). A segment counts in the measure it starts in. Each window's summed pitch-class weights are correlated (Pearson, in [-1, 1]) with 24 key profiles; a silent or flat window scores 0 for every key.
  - **Profiles.** Krumhansl-Kessler probe-tone profiles (Krumhansl & Kessler, "Tracing the dynamic changes in perceived tonal organization in a spatial representation of musical keys", Psychological Review 89, 1982, 334-368), configurable. The minor profile is the minor key as heard, so it gives the raised sixth and seventh weight. The profiles describe listeners' ratings of Western tonal music; they are a stated method, not a learned one, and fit chromatic music less well.
  - **The path.** One key per window by dynamic programming: cost = the sum of -correlation over windows + `change_penalty` (default 0.2, about the correlation gap between a clearly right key and its dominant or relative neighbour in one window) for each change. Deterministic: an exact tie keeps the previous key, then the lowest key index (the 12 major keys C to B, then the 12 minors). Window i labels the measure at its centre; the edge measures take the edge windows' keys; equal neighbours merge into a region, so every boundary is a barline and the regions are ordered and gap-free.
  - **Output.** Each region has a `Key` (`common_practice_build_key`, a Context id `<prefix>-<n>` and the common-practice-minimal-v1 `FrameworkReference`), a `TimeSpan`, first and last measure, a source (profile or key-signature) and an uncertainty, `1 - (r(chosen key) - r(best other key))` over the region's pooled weights, clamped to [0, 1] (1 when another key fits as well or better). Each change links two regions and carries a `KeyChangeExtent`, `is_return`, `cadence_confirmed` (always false here) and the pivot, if any.
  - **Extent.** Existing `KeyChangeExtent` values, chosen by the length and return of the region a change goes INTO: shorter than `tonicization_max_measures` (default 4) and followed by a return to the previous key is `TONICIZATION`; longer and returning is `WEAK_MODULATION`; not returning is `MODULATION`. The change back out of such an excursion is flagged `is_return` and carries the excursion's extent. `EXTENDED_TONICIZATION` is not used (it needs a chord count). No cadence is looked for, so `MODULATION` and `WEAK_MODULATION` here mean "a lasting new key region", not "a cadence established it"; cadence confirmation is later work (L3.3).
  - **Pivot chords.** At each change whose extent is a modulation, the pivot is the last segment at or before the boundary, up to `pivot_search_measures` (default 2) measures back, whose best chord from weighted identification (read in the old key and its own mode) lies inside the new key's natural collection; a minor key uses its relative major's collection, so a chord that needs the raised sixth or seventh is not diatonic there. A segment with no members or no matching chord is skipped. With none the change is direct (`has_pivot` false). The pivot is a segment index into the caller's list.
  - **Key signature boundaries.** A key signature event, snapped to the nearest barline, starts a span, and the key regions are found separately in each span, so no region straddles it. The FIRST region of a span that starts at an event is restricted to that signature's major key and its relative minor; after the first change every key is a candidate again, because a piece modulates inside one signature all the time. An event at the start restricts only the first region and forces no boundary; the same signature repeated is not a boundary; an event past the last measure is ignored; the mode byte is ignored. Such a region has source key-signature. Many exporters write a default C-major event at tick 0 whatever the key, which restricts the first region to C major or A minor.
  - **Harmony aware.** Three optional inputs, off when not given (details in `frameworks/common-practice-minimal/README.md`): the segment's identified chord as plain data, which adds functional evidence to a window's score (dominant to tonic motion into a tonic of the key's own mode, a leading tone resolving up, a raised seventh after minor colour in minor; `evidence_weight` 0.8); phrase boundary times, to which each region edge snaps within `snap_reach_measures` (1) measures; and a hold rule that folds a region built only on IV, V and ii of the key before it, with every tone diatonic to that key, back into that key (`hold_tolerance` 0.05). Phrase boundaries depend on cadences, which depend on keys, so the chains run two passes: key regions from the pitch classes alone, then chords, cadences and phrase ends from them, then key regions again with those chords and boundaries, and everything after them once more.
  - **Measured** (draft hand annotations, `tests/fixtures/annotations/clementi36_key_regions.txt` and `mozart545_key_regions.txt`, unverified and written before the code ran; defaults not tuned to them; reported by `tests/analysis/test_key_regions_corpus.c`). One pass, no chords: Clementi Op. 36 No. 1 (38 measures): 32 of 38 scored measures carry the annotated key (84.2%) and all 3 annotated modulation boundaries are found within 2 measures. Mozart K. 545, first movement (146 measures with the repeats written out, development unscored): 65 of 120 scored measures (54.2%) and 3 of the 5 boundaries (13, 29 and 41, not the recapitulation's 79 and 124), below the 80% target, so the test asserts 50% and those 3. With the first pass's chords as evidence the same test reports Clementi 33 of 38 (86.8%) and Mozart 71 of 120 (59.2%), information only. The remaining Mozart misses are the pattern the hold rule was written for, a subdominant or dominant stretch of 4 or more measures read as its own key (F major for the IV of measures 8-11, 36-39, 79-84 and 124-129; G major or A minor for the V and ii of 21-24 and 49-52), where the chord data of the first pass is itself wrong or incomplete (cadence recall 10%). On the chorale harness (ten chorales with RomanText analyses) the `key` metric is 69.1% (62.0% before), Roman numeral in the chain's key 52.0% (47.1%), chord root 84.5% (83.8%). On the Framework v1 reference pieces the keys checks stay at 14 pass / 22 deviations: Bach 114 regions 19-20 and 21-26 and the Clementi 36 modulation at 15 now match, while two Bach 114 changes now read as tonicizations because their regions are two measures long and no cadence confirms them (C3). Sensitivity to the change penalty (information only) is printed by the test; the default 0.2 stands.
  - **Known limits.** Windows of fixed length, so without a phrase boundary an edge can land a measure or two from where the music turns and a modulation shorter than about half a window is smoothed away; edges snap only to boundaries the first pass found, and the chord evidence is only as good as the chords; no cadence confirmation; the profile bias in chromatic music (a profile is a listener-rating average, so a passage that keeps changing key reads as the profile's best compromise); minor keys are the natural-minor collection for pivots; one key at a time, so a passage that is ambiguous between two keys reports the penalty-favoured one with high uncertainty; the signature restriction trusts the signature.

Both build their real `Chord`/`Key` via the framework, then unwrap it with `chord_take_interpretation`/`key_take_interpretation` (L6) into the bare `Interpretation` `AnalysisResult` carries -- `AnalysisResult` only ever holds `Interpretation`/`Evidence` objects, never a `Chord`/`Key` wrapper.

**`CadenceDetection` / `CadenceClassification` -- real root-motion rules, explicit caller-supplied refinements, no false-confidence guesses:**

`CadenceDetection` (`analysis/cadence_detection.h`) is the generic half: given a region and an already-weighted `UncertaintyCandidate` set, it picks the top candidate and reports it as a single `Interpretation` targeting the region (an existing L5 `Cadence` shell, `structure/cadence.h`) -- the same generic-Interpretation pattern `ChordIdentification`/`Motif`/`Theme`/`PhraseOccurrence` use; `CadenceDetection` does not itself get a new named output type. It now optionally accepts a `Framework`, threading a real `FrameworkReference` (e.g. to common-practice-minimal) into the Interpretation when supplied.

`CadenceClassification` (`analysis/cadence_classification.h`) is the real classifier: it builds that candidate set from chord ROOT MOTION -- each chord's scale-degree root (1-7), the same real, already-computed dimension the caller supplies to `ChordIdentification` itself, not a re-parse of chord label strings (a seventh chord's label, e.g. `"dominant7"`, carries no root information at all). Its rules:

- V(7) -> I root motion: authentic-cadence candidate.
- IV -> I: plagal-cadence candidate.
- ends on V: half-cadence (HC) candidate.
- V -> vi: deceptive-cadence candidate.
- anything else: a single `CADENCE_TYPE_UNKNOWN` candidate at full weight -- no guess.

Every candidate is a `CadenceType` (`semantics/cadence_type.h`), the one cadence vocabulary; the label the `Interpretation` carries comes from `cadence_type_label`, never from a hand-written string.

What's REAL vs. CALLER-SUPPLIED-REFINEMENT vs. UNCLASSIFIED, the same transparency standard `VoiceLeadingAnalysis`'s docs use:

- Real: the from/to root-degree motion driving which rule fires.
- Optional caller-supplied refinement (never required): `from_is_dominant_seventh` and Voice Leading's bass-motion classification (`analysis/voice_leading_analysis.h`) each only adjust the winning candidate's confidence tier within the authentic-cadence rule -- a caller with no Voice Leading data still gets a classification, just a lower-confidence one.
- Optional caller-supplied refinement that changes the LABEL, not just its weight: `soprano_resolves_to_tonic`, distinguishing PAC from IAC. `CadenceClassification` itself does not find the soprano: when the flag is absent, the authentic-cadence candidate is reported as `CADENCE_TYPE_AUTHENTIC_UNSPECIFIED` rather than guessing PAC or IAC. The real-music path is the standard-harmony analysis adapter (`frameworks/standard-harmony/analysis/analysis_cadence.h`, `docs/modules/standard-harmony-analysis.md`): it reads the soprano scale degree at the arrival from the outer voices, so PAC and IAC are distinguished there, and `AUTHENTIC_UNSPECIFIED` remains only when the soprano is absent at the arrival (with an uncertainty penalty). `CadenceClassification` and period detection are unchanged.

Competing readings (C7, C9; R-090, R-091, R-093, R-094): the adapter reports two readings, with shares in thousandths that sum to 1000, at two places. A root-position V(7) followed by I6 at a cadence point is a `SUBVERTED` (evaded) cadence or a prolongation of the dominant; a root-position V directly before a section start is a half cadence of the earlier section or the authentic cadence (PAC, IAC or unspecified) into the tonic that starts the next, which replaces the single half-cadence reading of R-038. Each is one `HarmonyCadenceResult` whose `share` is the primary reading's and whose `alternative` carries the other, with its own Interpretation (id of the primary's plus `-alt`, same Cadence target); the claims carry `reading`, `rank` and `share`. The shares are scored from evidence (a strong-beat arrival, a cadential approach, a following authentic cadence, a long dominant, a soprano rest, a PAC-shaped arrival) by weights in `HarmonyCadenceConfig` (`analysis_cadence_readings.h`), clamped to [50, 950]. The primary reading is the only one the accuracy harness and the phrase stage count; phrase-unit position is not used as evidence because phrases are built after cadences (L3.6).

**`PhraseBoundaryDetection` -- real cadence-primary/contour-rhythm-secondary boundary signal, built on `PhraseSegmentation`:**

`PhraseSegmentation` (`analysis/phrase_segmentation.h`) is unchanged and still just packages an already-determined `is_boundary` per point into an `Interpretation` targeting that point's event -- the same generic-Interpretation, caller-supplies-the-classification role `CadenceDetection` had before `CadenceClassification` was added on top of it.

`PhraseBoundaryDetection` (`analysis/phrase_boundary_detection.h`) is the real signal computation on top, the same delegation relationship `CadenceClassification` has to `CadenceDetection`: given a `NoteEvent` sequence annotated per point with `CadenceDetection`/`CadenceClassification` output (`has_cadence` + the winning candidate label as `cadence_type`, including `"unclassified"` as a legitimate value, not a gap) and Contour/RhythmicPattern discontinuity observations (`has_discontinuity`, e.g. a rest or a clear directional/rhythmic break -- still caller-supplied, the same scope boundary `MotifWindowMatching` hits), it derives real `is_boundary` values:

- Primary signal: any detected cadence at a point is a boundary, whatever its type.
- Secondary signal: a contour/rhythm discontinuity only counts as a boundary once `max_length_without_cadence` points (default `PHRASE_BOUNDARY_DETECTION_DEFAULT_MAX_LENGTH` = 8, flagged, not a Registry-settled rule) have elapsed since the last boundary with no cadence among them.

It then packages those `is_boundary` values into a `PhraseSegmentationPoint` array and delegates entirely to `phrase_segmentation_analyze` for that plain boundary `Interpretation` -- `phrase_segmentation.c` is not modified. Because `PhraseSegmentationClaim` has no room for which signal fired or which cadence type applied, `PhraseBoundaryDetection` reports that as a SEPARATE `Interpretation` (`phrase_boundary_detection_signal_analyze`, its own `PhraseBoundaryDetectionSignalClaim`: signal kind + cadence type when applicable), targeting the same event -- both Interpretations coexisting on the same target, the same competing/complementary-Interpretations principle `Chord`/`Key` already use, not a workaround.

Finally, `phrase_boundary_detection_build_occurrences` groups points between boundaries (boundary-inclusive) and constructs one `PhraseOccurrence` (L5, `structure/phrase_occurrence.h`) per phrase, spanning that phrase's event range -- a fresh `Phrase` (L4, `containers/phrase.h`) is constructed transiently per phrase purely to obtain the id `PhraseOccurrence`'s `Occurrence` targets, then destroyed, the same transient-construct-then-capture-id pattern `MotifDetection` uses for `MotifOccurrence`. Flagged design default: a trailing run of points with no closing boundary signal still becomes a final `PhraseOccurrence` (all material ends up in some phrase).

Out of scope for this branch (unchanged): Period/Sentence detection (a separate, later branch consuming `PhraseOccurrence` -- see `PeriodDetection` and `SentenceDetection` below), any pitch/interval math beyond what Contour/RhythmicPattern already provide, and any L9 query infrastructure (`PatternMatching`/`SimilarityModel`/`SearchResult`).

**`PeriodDetection` -- real cadence-strength antecedent/consequent pairing over consecutive `PhraseOccurrence`s, no new Structure/Occurrence type:**

Period/Sentence structures have no named type anywhere in the System Registry -- the TDD's only reference frames this as a plain `Interpretation` example (measures 1-8 = antecedent, framework = sentence-period theory), not a new `Structure`/`Occurrence` type. `PeriodDetection` (`analysis/period_detection.h`) follows that: no new named type, just a generic `Interpretation` whose claim describes the antecedent/consequent relationship, the same "no new named type" treatment `CadenceDetection`/`PhraseSegmentation` give their own generic Interpretations.

Given an ordered sequence of `PhraseOccurrence`s, each annotated with the `CadenceType` that triggered its boundary, `PeriodDetection` identifies a period pair between two consecutive phrases when the first's cadence ranks strictly weaker than the second's. The kernel does not own that order: the caller injects a ranking function (`PeriodDetectionRankFn`, `int (*)(CadenceType)`, larger is stronger, `<= 0` is not rankable) in `PeriodDetectionInput.rank` and in `period_detection_build_relationships`. `frameworks/standard-harmony` owns the order (`harmony_cadence_strength`, weakest to strongest: half < deceptive < plagal < authentic (unspecified) < imperfect authentic < perfect authentic; a subverted cadence ranks 0 and `CADENCE_TYPE_UNKNOWN` is unranked), so a caller passes it in:

```c
PeriodDetectionInput input = {entries, entry_count, harmony_cadence_strength};
```

Analysis code never includes a framework header. A pair where either phrase's cadence is unranked (`CADENCE_TYPE_UNKNOWN`, subverted, or invalid) is skipped entirely -- no Interpretation, no guess -- the same unknown-vs-low-confidence discipline `PhraseBoundaryDetection`/`CadenceClassification` already use.

Linking mechanism (the real design fork this branch had to resolve, not guess at): an `Interpretation`'s `target` is a single `EntityID`, but a period pair is two `PhraseOccurrence`s. `Relationship` (L6, `relationship/relationship.h`, `music-relationship`) is the TDD-documented device for a two-object link and, despite the System Registry marking it "Not Started," is fully implemented in code -- the same Registry-status-vs-code gap every analysis branch this session has found. `period_detection_build_relationships` builds one `Relationship` per identified pair (`source_id` = antecedent `PhraseOccurrence` id, `predicate_name` `"precedes"` -- in `PredicateDefinition`'s documented vocabulary, TDD §77 -- `target_id` = consequent `PhraseOccurrence` id). `period_detection_analyze` then emits one `Interpretation` per pair targeting that `Relationship`'s id (deterministically derived from the same phrase-id pair, so the two calls agree without one constructing the other's output), with a `PeriodDetectionClaim` (both phrase ids and cadence types, for convenience) and a 1/1 scalar-confidence `Uncertainty`. This is the first analysis-layer use of `Relationship`; `music-analysis` already depends on all four L6 modules, so no new dependency was introduced.

**`SentenceDetection` -- real statement/repetition/continuation chaining over `MotifDetection`'s matches, no new Structure/Occurrence type, extends `PeriodDetection`'s Relationship-chain pattern by one link:**

Sentence structure, like Period, has no named type anywhere in the System Registry -- `SentenceDetection` (`analysis/sentence_detection.h`) follows `PeriodDetection`'s exact "no new named type" treatment. But Sentence is a THREE-part structure (statement, repetition, continuation), not `PeriodDetection`'s two, and `Interpretation`'s `target` is still a single `EntityID` -- so this branch's first question (requirement 1) was whether Period's one-Relationship pattern extends. It does, by chaining two: `repetition --"transformed_from"--> statement` and `continuation --"transformed_from"--> repetition`, each with its own `Interpretation` (target = that `Relationship`'s id), both carrying a shared `SentenceDetectionClaim` recording all three occurrence ids. `"transformed_from"` is reused as-is from `Relationship`'s existing documented predicate vocabulary, not invented. Unlike `PeriodDetection`'s `"precedes"` (chronological, earlier -> later), `"transformed_from"` points derived -> source -- each part points at what it was transformed from, the predicate's own semantics deciding the direction rather than a copy of Period's chronological convention.

`SentenceDetection` consumes, not re-detects, the statement/repetition pair: the caller (who already ran `MotifDetection`/`MotifWindowMatching`) supplies each pair's two `MotifOccurrence` ids plus the statement's own `MotifWindow` (`Contour` + `RhythmicPattern` + event ids). What `SentenceDetection` adds is the third part: given a caller-sliced `continuation_candidate` window (the phrase's remaining material after the repetition, up to but not including its cadence -- `SentenceDetection` has no opinion on where that cadence falls or how to slice around it, the same no-opinion-on-boundary-placement stance `MotifWindowMatching` takes), it decides whether that candidate qualifies as a continuation via `SentenceContinuationMatching` (`analysis/sentence_continuation_matching.h`).

This branch's second question (requirement 2) was whether the existing `Fragmentation` transform (`transform/fragmentation.h`) could be reused for that decision. It cannot: `Fragmentation` only clones a caller-supplied `extracted_event_ids` set out of a source `Structure`'s events -- it has no comparison/matching logic of its own, so it cannot itself judge whether a given tail IS a fragment of the statement. `SentenceContinuationMatching` supplies that missing comparison instead, extending `MotifWindowMatching`'s exact-full-window-equality rule to a variable-length prefix match: a candidate qualifies iff its event count is >= 2 (a stated minimum-length floor, avoiding a single-note match) and strictly shorter than the statement's, AND its Contour direction sequence and RhythmicPattern duration sequence each equal the statement's over that same (shorter) prefix length. Equal Contour directions is transposition-invariant by construction (`Contour`, `semantics/contour.h`, carries direction only -- never pitch), the same fact `MotifWindowMatching`'s own doc comment already establishes for its exact match -- so a transposed continuation is matched by this one rule with no special case needed, confirmed by this branch rather than assumed.

Where no candidate continuation is supplied, or the supplied candidate does not qualify, `SentenceDetection` produces NO output for that pair (requirement 4) -- a phrase with no detected Sentence structure is a legitimate, common outcome (it may be a Period instead, or neither), never forced. `sentence_detection_build_relationships` mirrors `period_detection_build_relationships`'s split: `AnalysisResult` only ever holds `Interpretation`/`Evidence`, never `Relationship` objects, so a caller wanting the `Relationship`s themselves calls this in addition, getting matching ids (both mint deterministically from the same occurrence-id inputs, so neither call constructs the other's objects).

**`FormStructureDetection` -- real Section-boundary identification from structural-cadence strength over consecutive `PhraseOccurrence`s, no new Structure/Occurrence type:**

Unlike Period/Sentence, `Section` (L4, `containers/section.h`) already exists in code as a real, non-transient container -- `id` + `phrase_ids` (references into L4 `containers/phrase.h`). `FormStructureDetection` (`analysis/form_structure_detection.h`) mints a `Section` per identified group directly via `section_create`, no different from any other caller -- this is NOT `PhraseOccurrence`'s transient-mint-then-capture-id precedent, because there is nothing transient to discard here.

Given the same ordered, cadence-annotated `PhraseOccurrence` sequence `PeriodDetection` consumes, `FormStructureDetection` answers a coarser question: which cadences close a Section rather than merely a phrase. Flagged design default (requirement 2, not Registry-settled, deliberately narrower than `PeriodDetection`'s ranking): only `CADENCE_TYPE_PERFECT_AUTHENTIC` and `CADENCE_TYPE_IMPERFECT_AUTHENTIC` count as structural -- they are the only two cadence types that actually achieve tonic resolution. `CADENCE_TYPE_AUTHENTIC_UNSPECIFIED`, plagal, deceptive, and half all either substitute for or avoid landing on tonic, so they work as internal phrase punctuation but never as section-ending closure; an ambiguous PAC/IAC call is deliberately never promoted to structural, the same unknown-vs-low-confidence discipline used everywhere else in this codebase. A structural cadence at entry `i` (other than the last entry) closes a Section spanning entries since the previous boundary through `i`; a trailing run of entries after the last boundary that never reaches another structural cadence is NOT turned into a Section of its own -- only spans actually closed by a structural cadence become Sections (skip, don't guess, the same discipline `PeriodDetection`/`SentenceDetection` apply to their own pairs/chains). No structural cadence anywhere is a legitimate outcome: no boundary, no Section, no output at all (requirement 4).

Linking mechanism: reuses `PeriodDetection`'s exact pattern one level up. Consecutive identified Sections are linked by one `Relationship` each (`source_id` = earlier Section id, `predicate_name` `"precedes"`, `target_id` = later Section id -- the identical chronological semantics `PeriodDetection` already uses `"precedes"` for), then one `Interpretation` targeting that `Relationship`'s id, carrying a `FormStructureDetectionClaim` (both Section ids and the earlier Section's closing cadence type). `form_structure_detection_build_sections`/`form_structure_detection_build_relationships` mirror `PeriodDetection`'s split (`AnalysisResult` never owns Section/Relationship objects), minting matching ids from the same boundary computation so all three calls agree without one constructing another's objects.

**`ThematicReturnDetection` -- real thematic-return detection over `Theme`/`ThemeOccurrence` (L5, `structure/theme.h`/`structure/theme_occurrence.h`), no new Structure/Occurrence type, reuses `MotifWindowMatching`'s comparison rule at Theme granularity:**

Theme/ThemeOccurrence exist as L5 shells, Registry-marked "No (example only)" -- the same non-normative status `Motif` carries. `ThematicReturnDetection` (`analysis/thematic_return_detection.h`) follows the same "no new named type" treatment as every other analyzer in this module.

This branch's third question (requirement 3) was whether `MotifWindowMatching`'s exact Contour+RhythmicPattern comparison applies at Theme granularity, or whether Theme material is too long/varied and needs new matching logic. It applies directly, unmodified: `MotifWindowMatching`'s window-comparison rule is granularity-agnostic -- it compares whatever `Contour`/`RhythmicPattern` the caller built for a given window, with no assumption about window length. A `ThemeOccurrence`'s region (`region_event_ids`, potentially spanning several phrases) is exactly such a window; the caller builds one `MotifWindow` per `ThemeOccurrence` the same way every other `MotifWindow` caller already does. `ThematicReturnDetection` reimplements the comparison itself (contour direction equality AND rhythmic duration equality, element for element) rather than calling `motif_window_matching_find`, because that function only reports a per-window "did this match something" flag -- thematic return needs the actual matching PAIR (which later `ThemeOccurrence` recalls which earlier one), a shape `motif_window_matching_find`'s return value cannot express. The comparison rule is reused unchanged; only the pairwise bookkeeping differs, and `MotifWindowMatching`'s own public surface is untouched (out of scope for this branch).

Predicate choice (requirement 1): `"resembles"`, not `SentenceDetection`'s `"transformed_from"`. Derivation and recurrence are different claims -- `"transformed_from"` asserts a transformation lineage (X was transformed from Y); thematic return only asserts similarity with no claimed direction of derivation, which `"resembles"` (already in `PredicateDefinition`'s documented vocabulary, TDD §77) captures correctly. For every ordered pair of `ThemeOccurrence` entries (earlier, later) whose windows match, `ThematicReturnDetection` builds one `Relationship` (`source_id` = later `ThemeOccurrence` id, `predicate_name` `"resembles"`, `target_id` = earlier `ThemeOccurrence` id -- the recurrence claim is about the Theme material, not whatever Section it happens to sit in, the same structural-element-vs-container distinction `PeriodDetection`/`SentenceDetection` already draw) and one `Interpretation` targeting that `Relationship`'s id, carrying a `ThematicReturnDetectionClaim`. No two windows matching is a legitimate outcome: no output at all (requirement 4). `thematic_return_detection_build_relationships` mirrors the same `AnalysisResult`-never-owns-`Relationship`-objects split every other analyzer in this module uses.

Minimum window length (post-merge triage against `framework-v1-reference-set`, Bach BWV Anh. 114): manual GUI verification surfaced dense, near-total pairwise `"resembles"` matches among `ThemeOccurrence`s. Root cause confirmed: reusing `MotifWindowMatching`'s exact-match rule unchanged at Theme granularity, with no minimum-length floor, is too permissive one level up from Motif granularity -- short/generic `ThemeOccurrence` windows (e.g. a 3-event stepwise run) exact-match across unrelated occurrences even though they share no genuine recurring material. Not a window/segmentation mismatch and not `ThemeOccurrence` boundary mis-segmentation; the boundaries are fine, the bare comparison rule just has no length floor. Fix, local to `ThematicReturnDetection` only (`MotifWindowMatching`'s own behavior at Motif granularity is untouched, out of this branch's scope): any window below `THEMATIC_RETURN_DETECTION_MIN_WINDOW_LENGTH` (4 events) is excluded from matching entirely. Fixed constant, not `config`-driven -- `config` is unused everywhere in this analysis pass, so plumbing a threshold through it here would be a one-off pattern with no established shape to match. Same fixed-constant approach as `SentenceDetection`'s fixed min-2-events continuation floor and `PhraseBoundaryDetection`'s fixed `max_length_without_cadence = 8` default. 4 is a stated default, not Registry-settled: `SentenceDetection` already treats 2-event material as a legitimate minimal continuation, so 2 does not separate "genuine theme" from "incidental fragment" one level up; 4 gives real separation without a corpus-derived number to justify going higher -- flagged the same way `PeriodDetection`'s cadence-strength ordering is flagged, expected to move once more of the corpus is checked. `MotifWindowMatching`'s own `motif_window_matching_find` has the identical latent no-length-floor gap, just not visibly triggered because Motif-level callers already hand it short, curated candidate windows -- tracked separately as `fix/motif-detection-window-threshold`, not addressed here.

Windowed and varied matching (Phase P1, B7; supersedes the whole-window exact rule above, the 4-event floor stays): a return is no longer a whole theme repeating another note for note. For each pair of `ThemeOccurrence` windows the detector slides a stretch of `window_length` events (default 6, or the shorter theme's length when that is less, never below 4) across both and compares every pair of stretches with `motif_window_matching_compare` (`MotifWindowMatching`'s rule, now returning scores). The contour score is the share of equal up/down/same directions, the rhythm score the share of equal durations, each 0 to 1; the similarity of a stretch pair is the lower of the two, and the best stretch pair is the match. A pair is a return when its similarity reaches `threshold` (default 0.8). So a later theme that restates only the opening idea of an earlier one is found wherever the idea sits, a transposed statement matches (contour direction is transposition-invariant), and one changed duration or direction in six still matches while a different rhythm or shape does not. Both settings live in `ThematicReturnDetectionConfig` (`thematic_return_detection_default_config`, optional `config` field on `ThematicReturnDetectionInput`, `thematic_return_detection_build_relationships_configured`); a NULL config means the defaults. The default threshold is a stated default, not tuned: the Framework v1 returns are still blocked upstream (no L3.6 theme exists where the annotated returns are, C8), so the harness gives no threshold separation (identical results from 0.5 to 1.0). The evidence is recorded twice: `ThematicReturnDetectionClaim.similarity` on the Interpretation, whose confidence is the similarity in thousandths, and a `thematic-return-similarity` `Value` in the `qualifiers` of the "resembles" `Relationship` (`ThematicReturnSimilarity`: contour score, rhythm score, similarity, the two stretch offsets and the stretch length).

**Still placeholder/deferred (not touched by the common-practice-minimal wiring):**

- Owners ([analytic-job-owners.md](../analytic-job-owners.md)): `CadenceDetection` and `CadenceClassification` package caller candidates and are not the cadence detection owner (`harmony_analysis_find_cadences`); `VoiceLeadingAnalysis` packages caller-supplied motions and is not the motion classification owner (`standard_counterpoint_classify_motions`); `PhraseSegmentation` is an internal step of the kernel boundary detector, not the phrase segmentation owner (`standard_form_segment_phrases`); `ChordIdentification` and `KeyEstimation` are not the owners of chord identification (`common_practice_identify_weighted_sequence`) or key identification (`key_regions_estimate`) and live in the chain (`chain/README.md`); `MotifWindowMatching` is the exact contour and rhythm primitive under the motive matching owner (`motive_variant_recognize`).
- `VoiceLeadingAnalysis`, `ScaleAnalysis` -- unchanged; still report caller-supplied candidate sets rather than a framework-derived answer. `VoiceLeadingAnalysis` specifically has AnalysisRegression coverage pinning this packaging for parallel-fifth/parallel-octave motion and step/leap/same bass-motion shapes (`tests/analysis/test_voice_leading_analysis.c`) -- the classification itself is still caller-supplied, not derived.
- `MotifDetection` (the analyzer) was retired in P28 B4 (R-287): nothing called it. Its match-finding step (`analysis/motif_window_matching.h`) stays, with its own `MotifWindowOccurrence` result: given caller-supplied `MotifWindow`s (each a `Contour` + `RhythmicPattern` + event-id list -- window construction and segmentation stay the caller's job, same boundary Contour/RhythmicPattern themselves already draw), it finds matches by exact equality of Contour direction sequence AND RhythmicPattern duration sequence. Matching on direction alone is transposition-invariant by construction, so this one rule covers both exact repetition and transposition without any pitch-interval math. A match is reported only when a window matches at least one other (a motif needs >=2 occurrences by definition), and every match is reported at a fixed 1/1 confidence. Motive recognition of every other kind is `motive_variant_recognize` (`docs/modules/motive-variant-recognition.md`).
- Multi-candidate/confidence-weighted output for `ChordIdentification`/`KeyEstimation` -- deliberately out of scope for this pass; both analyzers are single-answer/deterministic today, matching the framework's own deterministic behavior. Planned as a later, separate refinement.
