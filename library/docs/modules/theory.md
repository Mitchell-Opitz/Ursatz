# theory

Layer L7 (`music-theory`). Path `src/theory/`, tests `tests/theory/`. Sibling of `rules`; unlike L6, `rules` depends directly on `theory`, but `theory` never depends back on `rules`.

## Provides

`Framework`, `Heuristic`, `Tendency`, `StatisticalModel`, `StyleModel`.

## Notes

- `Framework` holds no reference back into `interpretation`, and carries no forward reference to its own siblings (`Rule`, `Constraint`, `Preference`, `Heuristic`, `Tendency`, `StatisticalModel`, `StyleModel`) either. It is the thing an L6+ consumer resolves a reference against, never the thing that reaches back out. Dependency Spec Open Issue §7.1 is now closed. `Interpretation` carries an optional `FrameworkReference` (`core`, L1) rather than a live reference to this module; see `docs/modules/l6-notes.md`.
- `Tendency`'s frequency is not a Rule/Constraint verdict. `TendencyResult` is a disjoint enum from `RuleResult` and `ConstraintResult`; no function converts a `Tendency` into either, and no function in `rule.h`/`constraint.h` accepts a `Tendency`. An observed frequency (TDD §99) never automatically becomes a must/must-not/valid/invalid claim.
- `StatisticalModel`'s output cannot be silently promoted to a `Rule`. `theory/statistical_model.h` does not include `rules/rule.h`, and no function anywhere converts a `StatisticalModel` into a `Rule`. `training_corpus` is a plain string label (TDD §100), never a corpus object reference.
- `Heuristic` exposes no evaluate/pass-fail API at all. `theory/heuristic.h` is identity plus a label only (TDD §98); nothing on the type could be evaluated as true or false in the first place.
- `StyleModel` is shape only, same as `Tendency` and `StatisticalModel`. `style_features` is an opaque `Value`, `training_corpus` is a plain label string, and there is no fitting or comparison logic. Construction/shape tests only.

## Theory to Corpus open issue (Dependency Spec §7.2)

The System Registry lists `Tendency`, `StatisticalModel`, and `StyleModel` as depending on `Corpus` (L9, not yet implemented). This module does not implement that dependency; the spec is explicit that no implementation may establish it until the resolution is recorded upstream. `Tendency`, `StatisticalModel`, and `StyleModel` each accept corpus-derived statistics only as an opaque, already-computed `Value` supplied by the caller. No file under `src/theory/` or `src/rules/` includes a corpus header. `tests/theory/test_architecture_theory.c` and `tests/rules/test_architecture_rules.c` enforce this by allowlisting only L0-L6 directories.

## Deferred

Species-counterpoint frameworks (TDD Phase 10) and common-practice harmony (Roman numeral analysis, harmonic function, voice leading, cadence, tonicization, modulation, TDD Phase 11) are built as extensions on top of `Framework`/`Rule`/`Constraint` after this module, never as changes to the L7 kernel itself.

The first such extension, `common-practice-minimal-v1`, is now built at `frameworks/common-practice-minimal/` (physically outside `src/`, its own architecture-gate test at the same L0-L6-plus-`Framework`/`Rule`/`Constraint` dependency ceiling `Framework` itself carries). It covers the seven diatonic triads, four diatonic seventh-chord qualities (dominant7, major7, minor7, half-diminished7), and major/natural-minor keys; see that module's own `README.md` for its exact scope and exclusions. This establishes the `frameworks/<name>/` location as the pattern future extensions (jazz harmony, species counterpoint, etc.) are expected to follow.

## Dependencies

Depends on `identity` (L0), `core` (L1), `time`/`pitch` (L2), `events` (L3), `containers` (L4), `structure` (L5), and all four L6 modules only.

A second extension, `standard-notation-v1`, is built at `frameworks/standard-notation/` with the same shape and dependency ceiling. It covers the conventions of Western staff notation: the five-line staff, clef-to-pitch assignment, ledger lines, stem direction, and the drawing of seconds. Its rules are built on the `Clef` and `StaffPosition` value types in `src/pitch/`; see that module's `README.md` for scope and exclusions.

A third notation-adjacent extension, `standard-tonality-v1`, is built at `frameworks/standard-tonality/` with the same shape and ceiling. It covers the tonal conventions of Western music: the major scale (W-W-H-W-W-W-H, named for its first note), the natural, harmonic, and melodic minor scales, scale degrees with movable-do solfege and degree names (including me/le/te and the subtonic), which major or minor key a key signature belongs to, parallel and relative keys, and the circle of fifths with real and imaginary keys and its minor ring. It also holds the interval rules built on `GenericInterval` (`src/pitch/`): size names and compound intervals, quality by the major-scale method (perfect, major, minor, augmented, diminished, and doubly and triply altered), inversion, melodic and harmonic consonance, the white-key method, and enharmonic equivalence of intervals; consonance is this framework's table and varies with milieu. Triads follow: structure and the four qualities, spelling from root and quality, identification from any notes (doublings and spacing aside), chord symbols (parse, format, and a `Chord` builder), and triad quality by scale degree in major and minor keys. Seventh chords follow the same shape: structure and the five qualities with their alternate names, spelling, identification, chord symbols including the slash bass, and quality by scale degree in major and minor keys. Roman numerals follow: numerals for triads and sevenths (case, circle, plus, and half-diminished symbols, with major and dominant sevenths told apart by degree), key labels, and the numeral of any chord in a major or minor key, with a `Chord` builder. common-practice-minimal's own triad labels are unchanged. Its absolute chord symbols coexist with common-practice-minimal's key-relative Roman-numeral triads, which are unchanged. It also holds the seven diatonic modes (patterns, brightness order, alterations, modal solfege), the major and minor pentatonic collections, and the chromatic scale with its raised and lowered solfege. How accidentals are ordered and placed in a key signature is notation and stays in `standard-notation-v1`. Scale-degree vocabulary is a typed set here, while `ScaleDegree` and `Key` in `src/` keep their labels opaque; `standard_tonality_build_scale_degree` builds a populated `ScaleDegree`. It uses 12-TET semitone arithmetic of its own rather than depending on standard-notation. A minor key rests on the natural minor scale; picking the major or minor key from a signature's two candidates is a judgement left to key identification. `common-practice-minimal` is unchanged.

Another extension, `standard-texture-v1`, is built at `frameworks/standard-texture/` with the same shape and ceiling. It covers musical texture: the four main categories (monophony, heterophony, homophony, polyphony), homophony's two subcategories (homorhythm, melody and accompaniment), classifying a passage from features the caller has measured (every texture it has, or one by a fixed precedence), and works that move between textures (change points by texture or by category, a mixed check). Its names are the vocabulary for `Texture`, the framework-relative interpretation of a passage built in `src/interpretation/`. Reading the features from events is analysis, not a rule here.

Another extension, `standard-voicing-v1`, is built at `frameworks/standard-voicing/` with the same shape and ceiling. It covers writing a chord in strict SATB style, with the six rules a four-part chord follows: stem direction (staff and up/down stems by voice), chord construction (the bass matches the inversion a Roman numeral figure indicates, no missing or foreign notes), range, spacing (an octave between upper voices, a twelfth tenor to bass), voice crossing, and doubling (the leading tone and chordal sevenths are never doubled, a seventh chord doubles nothing, and a triad usually doubles its bass as a soft preference). An aggregate check reports which rules a voicing breaks. It is built on the `VoicePart` value type in `src/semantics/`. The chord a voicing realizes (its tones, inversion, and leading tone) is supplied by the caller, so the module does not depend on standard-tonality. Voice leading between chords is not covered.

Another extension, `standard-counterpoint-v1`, is built at `frameworks/standard-counterpoint/` with the same shape and ceiling. It covers strict two-part counterpoint in the species tradition: the consonance categories (perfect consonance, imperfect consonance, dissonance, with the perfect fourth dissonant against the lowest part), the five types of two-part motion built on the `MotionType` value type in `src/semantics/`, the rules for a well-formed cantus firmus, melodic writing rules (clausula vera, forbidden leaps and outlines, limits on repeats, leaps, and the climax), harmonic writing rules (first and last interval, gap, voice crossing, parallel fifths and octaves, direct fifths and octaves, interior unisons), and Huron's melodic tendencies as soft preferences. Rules about what a line must be are hard; rules written as limit, avoid, or prefer are soft. An aggregate check reports which groups an exercise breaks as hard and soft bit masks. The lines, the cantus firmus tonic, and the limits are supplied by the caller. A first-species layer (`CounterpointSpecies` in `src/semantics/`) stacks stricter rules on the common ones for note against note counterpoint: all intervals consonant, no repeated perfect consonances or direct fifths and octaves, the counterpoint's beginning on do or sol and its ending (the clausula vera), independence of the parts (limited repeats, voice overlap, the gap), motion variety, and the counterpoint line held to cantus firmus quality. A second-species layer (two notes against one, built on `BeatStrength` in `src/semantics/`) models the counterpoint as slots, two per bar: strong beats are always consonant and follow the first-species principles between bars, weak beats are consonant (following one of seven patterns) or dissonant passing tones, and the beginning and ending, the line's leaps, and the secondary climaxes have their own rules. A third-species layer (four notes against one, built on `BeatStrength` with its medium strength and the `NonChordTone` value type in `src/semantics/`) uses four slots per bar (strong, weak, medium, weak): downbeats follow the second-species principles with their own limits on repeated perfect intervals, dissonance is allowed on beats 2 to 4 only as a passing tone, a pair of passing tones, a neighbor tone, a double neighbor, or a nota cambiata, and the line has its own leap, stepwise, leap placement, and climax rules. A fourth-species layer (syncopation, built on the `NonChordTone` suspension in `src/semantics/`) uses two slots per bar with the weak-beat note tied across the bar line: tied downbeats are prepared, suspended, and resolved by step down (7-6, 4-3, and 9-8 above the cantus firmus, 2-3 below it, or a consonant tie), weak beats are consonant and follow first-species principles between bars, species may be broken in a bar by a new note on the downbeat under second-species principles, the cantus firmus ends re to do over a tied do and ti and a final whole note, and the line has its own leap, stepwise, and leading tone rules. A fifth-species layer (florid counterpoint, with the anticipation added to the `NonChordTone` value type in `src/semantics/`) models the counterpoint on an eighth-note grid, eight slots per bar: whole, half, dotted half, quarter, and paired eighth notes mix in any bar, with syncopation across the bar line, dissonance only as a passing tone, neighbor, double neighbor, nota cambiata, suspension, or anticipation, downbeats that follow the earlier species, and a clausula vera into a last whole note. The species end at the fifth.

Four more extensions follow the same shape and ceiling. `standard-markings-v1` (`frameworks/standard-markings/`) gives meaning to the Italian marking vocabulary carried by the opaque `Dynamics`, `Articulation`, `TempoMarking`, and `StructuralMarker` types: dynamics order and -issimo stacking, articulation classes, tempo grouping and order, and repeat/ending/D.C./D.S. playback order. The tempo order is the conventional one, asserted as ordinal rank with no bpm ranges, and repeat signs are not retaken after a D.C./D.S. jump unless `take_repeats_after_jump` is set. `standard-rhythm-v1` (`frameworks/standard-rhythm/`) gives `NoteValue` and `Tuplet` their notation meaning: American and British names, the halving hierarchy, notehead fill, stems and flags, rests, dots, ties, and tuplets (triplet 3:2, duplet 2:3, the written number, the span). Ties are checked from plain pitches and values, since the `Tie` entity holds only two NoteEvent ids, and pitch is compared as written. `standard-meter-v1` (`frameworks/standard-meter/`) reads a `TimeSignature` as a simple or compound meter: classification into six categories (an unusual top number such as 5 is unclassified, not invalid), beats per measure, beat division, the dotted compound beat, downbeat and upbeat, measures and multi-measure rests, anacrusis, American counting for both classes, and beaming and beat-boundary ties built on a shared beat grid, borrowed divisions (triplets and duplets, their level and counting), hypermeter, and syncopation. It depends on `time/` types, not on standard-rhythm. Quadruple feeling like two groups of two is perceptual and is not asserted. `standard-form-v1` (`frameworks/standard-form/`) holds the conventions of phrase-level form for the `FormLevel` and `MotiveKind` names: the hierarchy of form levels (which may collapse, so it is a guide), motives (shorter than their idea, pitch by default, with the usual transformations mapped to `src/transform/` types), phrases (beginning, middle, end, closure usually by a cadence), and the four steps of a segmentation analysis with the idea-length convention. It also covers the phrase archetypes: sentence (presentation, continuation with fragmentation and its other traits, the sentential minimum and the archetypal checklist), period (antecedent and consequent, the consequent ending with the stronger cadence), the repeated phrase (same cadence, written out), the compound period, and unique forms as the other end of a spectrum. Cadence strength is this framework's own ordering, not standard-harmony's. Segmentation, sentence and period are procedures over `Phrase`, `Subphrase`, `Idea` and `Cadence`, not entities. `western-periods-v1` (`frameworks/western-periods/`) holds the six conventional stylistic periods; their boundaries overlap and a year lookup returns every period containing it.
